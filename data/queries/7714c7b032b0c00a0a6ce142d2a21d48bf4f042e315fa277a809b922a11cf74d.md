# Query
parliaments prorogation

# Response
As of November 9, 2026, India’s Lok Sabha had gone **88 days without prorogation** after it adjourned *sine die* (without setting a date to meet again). Prorogation formally ends the session. Congress leader Jairam Ramesh alleged that the delay gave Home Minister Amit Shah time to secure a two-thirds majority for the Delimitation Bills. The indexed report does not independently establish the reason for the delay. [Lok Sabha](data/index/www_thehindu_com/lok_sabha/084c597c18e446082583357dc1d4d4e78965716a8fb2c013719390b08109b519.md) · [Jairam Ramesh](data/index/www_thehindu_com/jairam_ramesh/084c597c18e446082583357dc1d4d4e78965716a8fb2c013719390b08109b519.md)

Ramesh said *Practice and Procedure of Parliament* puts the usual interval at **two to four days** and cites a **28-day** gap in 2015. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju cited an **87-day** gap in 2008, which Ramesh said had been agreed to by all parties.
