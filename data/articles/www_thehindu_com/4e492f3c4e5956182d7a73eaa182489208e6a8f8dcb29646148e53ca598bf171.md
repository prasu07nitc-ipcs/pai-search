## Title

Rubio says Trump's war against Iran not 'necessarily popular' but needed to stop a nuclear threat

## Description

Rubio defends Trump’s Iran war as necessary to curb nuclear threats, despite unpopularity and political backlash.

## Body

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Sunday (November 7, 2026) that President Donald Trump launched the war against Iran, even if it was not “necessarily popular”, to safeguard the world from the country's nuclear ambitions.

He would not comment directly about the Republican wipeout in Tuesday's (November 3) elections, which delivered a resounding rebuke to Mr. Trump. The unpopularity of the Iran war and its impact on gasoline prices and other parts of the economy helped Democrats seize control of Congress.

Asked about Vice-President J.D. Vance's social media post last week that largely blamed the war for the Republican losses, Mr. Rubio told reporters that Mr. Trump “identified a very real threat” in Iran and worked to stop the Islamic Republic from pursuing a nuclear weapon.

“He addressed it even though oftentimes the right thing to do for the country — confronting difficult challenges for the security of our country — doing it isn't necessarily popular, but necessary,” Mr. Rubio said in Florida before heading to a Group of 7 Foreign Ministers meeting in Paris.

America's top diplomat offered a robust defence of the more than eight-month-old war even as voters blamed Mr. Trump's policies for the hit to their pocketbooks, including higher energy prices that stemmed from disruptions to shipments in the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial waterway where one-fifth of the world's oil transited before the war.

Mr. Rubio's comments also put him at odds with Mr. Vance amid speculation of both of their 2028 presidential ambitions. Mr. Trump told reporters on Sunday (November 7) that Mr. Vance was right — other than that his Vice-President should have also insisted it was the correct choice to go to war.

“I can tell you, this historically — for history — will go down as one of the best things we ever did," Mr. Trump said. "Because if they ever had a nuclear weapon, they would blow up the world."

Iran has long maintained that its nuclear programme is peaceful. According to the International Atomic Energy Agency, Iran has 440.9 kg of uranium that is enriched up to 60% purity, a short, technical step from weapons-grade levels of 90%.

With no clear administration strategy to wind down the conflict and West Asia on edge over the possibility of a return to major hostilities, mediators are trying to revive talks between the sides.

Mr. Rubio said, “I wouldn't say we're at the verge of a breakthrough.”

He reiterated that Mr. Trump prefers a diplomatic solution, but “right now, it hasn't been very productive because the Iranian demands are simply not just unacceptable, but in some cases outrageous and ridiculous and really not realistic.”

*The Associated Press* has reported that Iran was reviewing proposals presented by chief mediator Qatar aimed at addressing differences with the United States about Tehran's nuclear program. But Iran was seen as unwilling to negotiate with the Trump administration over their atomic activities until the U.S agrees to several core conditions that Tehran has been demanding be part of any ceasefire agreement, officials have said.

Those include ending the U.S. blockade on Iran's ports, lifting sanctions, unfreezing its assets, issuing compensation for war damage and ending hostilities throughout the region, including Israeli operations in Lebanon.

While Mr. Rubio said there was no diplomatic breakthrough at hand, Mr. Trump told *CNN* in a phone interview on Sunday (November 7) that as long as the U.S. sees progress in negotiations with Tehran and they sign a “strong agreement,” it's “highly unlikely it's going to be necessary” to resume strikes.

Mr. Trump said, however, that the U.S. had identified infrastructure targets, including 38 bridges, that it would strike if military action resumes.

The U.S. military, meanwhile, still has carried out limited strikes on Iran, including against its air defences, at least twice over the past week. It also fired on and disabled a commercial ship on Saturday (November 6) that the U.S. says was trying to breach its naval blockade on Iranian ports.

Mr. Rubio acknowledged that the U.S. will take defensive actions if needed.

“If there's a clear and present threat to shipping or to our forces in the region, we have a right to defend ourselves,” Mr. Rubio said.
