# [Prabhakar challenges Shah’s SIR claims, questions implications for earlier elections](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/telangana/parakala-prabhakar-questions-if-governments-elected-post-2014-were-illegal/article71660336.ece)
*Updated: 2026-11-03T17:27:47+05:30*

Economist and political commentator Parakala Prabhakar has challenged Union Home Minister Amit Shah’s description of three crore deleted electoral entries as belonging to Bangladeshi infiltrators, bringing the dispute over voter-roll revision back to the evidence behind the removals.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/7asqqp/article71660500.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/DSC2289.jpg)

## A challenge over earlier election results

At a November 3 press meeting organised by the Telangana Voter Rights Forum, Prabhakar asked what Shah’s assertion would imply for governments elected in 2014, 2019 and 2024. His challenge was a political argument about the consistency of the minister’s position, rather than an established finding that those elections were unlawful. [The Hindu’s supplied report](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/telangana/parakala-prabhakar-questions-if-governments-elected-post-2014-were-illegal/article71660336.ece) records him urging fresh elections if Shah’s premise were accepted.

Prabhakar also warned that the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) could exclude marginalised voters. Academic and activist G. Haragopal called for the exercise to be scrapped, arguing that declining confidence in elections threatens democracy, according to the same report.

## What Shah said in Hyderabad

Speaking at Hyderabad’s Mozamjahi Market on November 2, Shah defended the removals and accused the Opposition of protecting infiltrators. He attributed roughly 13 crore deletions to relocation, deaths, duplicate registrations and three crore entries he associated with Bangladeshi infiltrators. These were the minister’s claims, as reported by [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/amit-shah-sir-remarks-infiltrators-voter-rolls-india-bloc-rally-congress-rahul-gandhi-10947654/).

Shah also argued that Narendra Modi’s victories in 2014, 2019 and 2024 preceded the SIR, rejecting suggestions that the revision explained the BJP’s earlier electoral success. He challenged Rahul Gandhi to a special parliamentary discussion on the revision, infiltrators and electronic voting machines.

[South First’s November 2 report](https://thesouthfirst.com/telangana/amit-shah-says-sir-removed-3-crore-illegal-immigrants-vows-bjp-rule-in-telangana/) said the Election Commission had not released a figure for foreign nationals identified on the rolls. It also reported Telangana minister Ponnam Prabhakar demanding evidence for Shah’s assertion. Neither report establishes that three crore deleted entries represent adjudicated cases of foreign citizenship.

## Electoral exclusion and citizenship are distinct questions

The distinction matters in the judicial background to the controversy. In its May 27 coverage of the Supreme Court’s Bihar SIR judgment, [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/sir-judgment-live-updates-supreme-court-deliver-verdict-10709840/) reported that the court upheld the revision while stressing that an electoral determination does not finally settle citizenship. Cases involving citizenship doubts were to go to the competent authority, with notice and an opportunity to be heard.

That reported ruling provides context for the present disagreement: removal from an electoral roll and a final determination that someone is not an Indian citizen are different decisions.
