# [ECI defends CEC in court as INDIA bloc prepares October 30 symbolic proceeding and November 1 rally](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/india-bloc-public-impeachment-cec-gyanesh-kumar-amid-protest/article71621030.ece)
*Updated: 2026-10-25T06:33:51+05:30*

The Election Commission has defended Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s functioning before the Supreme Court, as opposition parties prepare an October 30 “public impeachment” programme and a November 1 march to Delhi’s Ramlila Maidan. The planned public event is a political mobilisation; it cannot itself remove the CEC from office.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/hi6tji/article71621054.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/20261005428L.jpg)

## Court defence follows the opposition announcement

In a counter-affidavit filed on October 26, the ECI said its three members had unanimously approved the disputed special intensive revision (SIR) orders and denied amending the statutory Form 6 used for voter registration. It opposed a plea to restrain Kumar, invoking the constitutional protection governing the CEC’s removal. These are the Commission’s submissions rather than findings resolving the petitions. [Moneycontrol’s October 26 account](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/newspaper-report-not-evidence-how-eci-is-defending-sir-and-form-6-row-in-supreme-court-14060521.html) reported that the matter was listed for October 27; no hearing outcome was established in the sources inspected.

The affidavit followed Congress general secretary K.C. Venugopal’s October 24 announcement that INDIA bloc parties would hold a public proceeding against Kumar on October 30 and then organise a “Dilli Chalo” march ending at Ramlila Maidan on November 1. The opposition is pressing allegations of electoral irregularities and demanding Kumar’s removal, according to [The Hindu’s original account](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/india-bloc-public-impeachment-cec-gyanesh-kumar-amid-protest/article71621030.ece).

## Two planned events in Delhi

Venugopal said Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge had asked INDIA bloc partners to bring their leaders and members to Delhi. His projection that at least one million people would join the November 1 gathering is an organiser’s expectation for a future event, not an established attendance figure. [Moneycontrol’s October 24 report](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/india-bloc-to-hold-public-impeachment-proceeding-against-cec-gyanesh-kumar-on-october-30-mega-rally-on-november-1-14059261.html) also described preparations and coordination meetings at the Congress headquarters.

[The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Oct/24/india-bloc-to-hold-mock-impeachment-of-cec-gyanesh-kumar-on-oct-30-at-delhis-ashoka-hotel) reported that the October 30 programme was planned at the Ashoka Hotel lawns. CPI general secretary D. Raja told the newspaper it would take place in the afternoon. An opposition leader said an earlier October 28 plan had changed because a suitable venue was unavailable.

The newspaper described plans to invite legal experts, judges and members of the public to discuss the opposition’s case. Those reported invitations and arrangements remain plans ahead of the event.

## What the public proceeding can do

The organisers’ use of “public impeachment” describes a symbolic forum for presenting their allegations and seeking public support. The legal safeguard is separate: Section 11(2) of the [2023 Act governing election commissioners](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/19721/1/a2023-49.pdf) protects the CEC from removal except through the manner and grounds applicable to a Supreme Court judge.

The original [Hindu report](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/india-bloc-public-impeachment-cec-gyanesh-kumar-amid-protest/article71621030.ece) notes that Article 324(5) governs that protection and distinguishes it from the Constitution’s use of “impeachment” for the President under Article 61. A public meeting therefore does not amount to the constitutional removal process. The ECI’s court response and the opposition’s planned mobilisation address the controversy through different institutions, while the allegations remain contested.
