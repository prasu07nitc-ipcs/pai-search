# [Supreme Court seeks protest organisers’ response to Delhi traders’ plea, stresses balance with livelihoods](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/demonstrations-generate-protest-economy-supreme-court-tells-delhi-traders/article71660312.ece)
*Updated: 2026-11-03T17:17:31+05:30*

The Supreme Court on November 3, 2026, directed Delhi traders challenging demonstrations to bring protest organisers into the proceedings. The hearing highlighted competing livelihoods: businesses reporting disruption and vendors who earn from protest gatherings.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/z40aih/article71660413.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/CONGRESS%20INDIA%20BLOC%20RALLY%20DELHI%202026%2049.JPG)

## Organisers must be heard

The Kirana Committee Delhi must add the organisers accused of damaging business as parties to its petition, [LiveLaw reported](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/anti-cec-protests-supreme-court-directs-traders-to-add-organisers-as-parties-in-plea-over-loss-of-business-557064). The direction came during hearings concerning protests against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and related complaints about policing and surveillance.

The court sought replies from the authorities on the traders’ case, according to [The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Nov/03/cant-occupy-a-site-at-all-times-sc-hears-traders-plea-against-ramlila-maidan-protest). The supplied account from [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/demonstrations-generate-protest-economy-supreme-court-tells-delhi-traders/article71660312.ece) says the bench declined interim relief before hearing the organisers.

## Two sides of the economic argument

Counsel for traders sought a two-month pause in demonstrations, arguing that festive-season disruption threatened sales. Those submissions were allegations of economic harm, rather than findings establishing the scale of losses. LiveLaw also reported a counterargument from senior advocate N. Hariharan that police barricades were responsible for business disruption.

Chief Justice Surya Kant said access to a designated protest venue could not be monopolised indefinitely: other groups also needed an opportunity to demonstrate. He emphasised clear conditions and time limits in protest permissions, [according to LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/anti-cec-protests-supreme-court-directs-traders-to-add-organisers-as-parties-in-plea-over-loss-of-business-557064).

The Hindu’s supplied report adds that Justice Joymalya Bagchi described a “protest economy”, pointing to vendors supplying food and water. The observation broadened the livelihood question beyond nearby shops; it did not establish that vendors’ gains offset traders’ claimed losses.

## Surveillance remains a separate dispute

The supplied Hindu account also describes arguments over facial recognition and privacy at demonstrations. The government defended surveillance on security grounds, while Justice Bagchi raised the need to consider reasonable expectations of privacy. These were arguments and observations during the hearing; the account does not establish a final ruling on the technology’s legality.
