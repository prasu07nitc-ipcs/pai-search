# [Tasmac disruption continues as ₹10 bottle-return change sparks staff protests](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/queues-and-chaos-at-tasmac-outlets-as-10-price-hike-bottle-buyback-scheme-trigger-confusion/article71657895.ece)
*Updated: 2026-11-03T05:00:00+05:30*

Tamil Nadu’s revised liquor-bottle charge has triggered staff protests and disrupted sales, with the government reporting disciplinary action as customers queue at outlets that remain open.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/on5uec/article71658192.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/BVR_1704.JPG)

## Notices and suspensions as disruption continues

More than 960 Tasmac employees have received show-cause notices and four have been suspended for allegedly obstructing shop openings, Prohibition and Excise Minister K Vignesh said, according to [The New Indian Express’s November 3 report](https://www.newindianexpress.com/cities/coimbatore/2026/Nov/03/10-tasmac-outlets-shut-on-day-two-of-stir-notice-to-960-staff-four-suspended). He said alternative staff would be deployed. The newspaper reported that about 460 outlets remained closed on Monday, November 2, while unions said their protest would continue because talks had not taken place.

In Chennai and neighbouring districts, customers waited for hours in rain, fearing further closures, according to [The Hindu’s supplied report](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/queues-and-chaos-at-tasmac-outlets-as-10-price-hike-bottle-buyback-scheme-trigger-confusion/article71657895.ece). It described social-media rumours adding to uncertainty before Deepavali. Actual protest closures and rumours about wider shutdowns are distinct parts of the disruption.

## What changed in the ₹10 charge

From November 1, the environmental and social welfare cess puts ₹10 into the bottle’s retail price, replacing the separate ₹10 collection under the earlier buyback arrangement. Customers can recover ₹10 by returning empty bottles. The minister presented the change as a way to account for receipts through government records, [The New Indian Express reported on October 31](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/tamil-nadu/2026/Oct/31/tasmac-to-levy-rs-10-green-cess-per-bottle-from-november-1).

The distinction matters: the printed price rises, but the new arrangement replaces a separate collection that already existed. The refund remains linked to returning the bottle.

## Storage and scanning complaints

Employees say shops lack room for returned bottles and that the collection work adds to their workload. [DT Next’s November 1 report](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/tasmac-shops-shut-in-several-districts-as-workers-protest-bottle-buy-back-scheme) described demands for outsourcing or automated collection machines as the flash strike began.

The Hindu’s supplied account adds complaints about outdated price stickers, malfunctioning scanners and payment terminals, and little preparation time. These are staff accounts of implementation difficulties. The sources reviewed do not establish that operations have since returned to normal.
