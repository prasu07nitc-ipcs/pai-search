# [ECI invokes CEC removal safeguards while defending collective decisions on voter-roll revision](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/cec-can-be-removed-only-like-a-supreme-court-judge-eci-tells-top-court/article71628290.ece)
*Updated: 2026-10-27T09:26:39+05:30*

The Election Commission has opposed efforts to restrain Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, while maintaining that its three members collectively approved the disputed special intensive revision of electoral rolls. The submissions set out the Commission’s defence; they do not settle the allegations before the Supreme Court.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/oq1800/article71628425.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/SIR%20ELECTION%20COMMISSION%20PC%20%20DELHI%202025_27_10_2.jpg)

## Removal protection and scrutiny of decisions

A joint affidavit filed on October 26, 2026, on behalf of the ECI, Kumar and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi argues that the CEC cannot be removed except through the procedure and grounds applicable to a Supreme Court judge. It challenges the evidentiary basis of petitions relying on reporting about internal disagreements, according to [The Hindu’s account](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/cec-can-be-removed-only-like-a-supreme-court-judge-eci-tells-top-court/article71628290.ece).

Section 11(2) of the [2023 law governing the commissioners](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/19721/1/a2023-49.pdf) expressly preserves that removal safeguard. Separately, Section 18 requires unanimity as far as possible in Commission business and a majority decision when members disagree.

The court had issued notice on October 5 and declined to suspend the CEC through an ex parte interim order. The matter was listed for October 27; no outcome of that hearing was established in the sources inspected for this article. [Moneycontrol’s report](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/newspaper-report-not-evidence-how-eci-is-defending-sir-and-form-6-row-in-supreme-court-14060521.html) describes the affidavit as preliminary and says the Commission sought liberty to provide a fuller response if required.

## The Commission’s account of collective approval

The ECI says the SIR orders dated June 24, 2025, October 27, 2025, and May 14, 2026, received unanimous approval from all three commissioners. It also says that the counter-affidavit itself was unanimously approved, [Mint reported](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/sir-controversy-election-commission-says-all-three-members-approved-orders-denies-amending-form-6-11793031937198.html).

According to [The Hindu’s account](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/cec-can-be-removed-only-like-a-supreme-court-judge-eci-tells-top-court/article71628290.ece), the affidavit describes files passing through both Election Commissioners before reaching the CEC, with approvals recorded and signed. Urgent approvals may be obtained electronically and subsequently entered in the official file.

The same account reports that Sandhu and Joshi wrote separately to the Cabinet Secretary on July 29 about an officer’s conduct following a July 28 internal work-allocation order. The Commission says that order was never implemented. Those exchanges remain part of the disputed background rather than a judicial finding about the legality of the SIR decisions.

## Form 6 and voter applications

The Commission denies changing Form 6, the statutory application for electoral-roll inclusion. It characterises the additional SIR declaration as a separate document that helps establish links with earlier rolls. [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/newspaper-report-not-evidence-how-eci-is-defending-sir-and-form-6-row-in-supreme-court-14060521.html) reports that the affidavit also describes a change from October 21, 2026, separating Form 6 and the declaration in the online application process.

The ECI says booth-level officers can collect documents at applicants’ homes and that ordinary enrolment remains available after the SIR, according to [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/sir-controversy-election-commission-says-all-three-members-approved-orders-denies-amending-form-6-11793031937198.html). These are the Commission’s explanations of its process, while the challenges to its functioning remain for the court to examine.
