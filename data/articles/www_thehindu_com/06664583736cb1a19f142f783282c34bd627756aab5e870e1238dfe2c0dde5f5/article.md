# [Tamil Nadu minister cites 15% growth in retained GST revenue amid collections debate](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/tn-economic-performance-cannot-be-assessed-merely-by-gross-gst-revenue-commercial-taxes-minister/article71660464.ece)
*Updated: 2026-11-03T17:18:50+05:30*

Tamil Nadu Commercial Taxes and Registration Minister D. Logesh Tamilselvan says the State’s GST revenue after interstate settlements rose 15% in the current financial year through October, arguing that gross collections alone give an incomplete picture of its finances and economy.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/incoming/9mpql3/article71660749.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/DSC_0945.JPG)

## Two measures behind the political dispute

Responding on November 3 to Opposition criticism of Tamil Nadu’s gross GST performance, Tamilselvan also cited 8% growth in post-settlement receipts for October alone. [DT Next’s report](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/tamilnadu/tamil-nadus-post-settlement-gst-revenue-grows-15-till-october-says-minister) attributes both figures to the minister’s statement; they are reported government claims rather than an independent calculation presented here.

His distinction concerns where tax is initially collected and how much revenue subsequently belongs to the State. Gross collections include State GST, Central GST and Integrated GST. Tamilselvan said the full IGST amount collected in Tamil Nadu cannot be treated as its revenue because interstate tax is apportioned among States. The measure he highlighted combines SGST and the IGST settlement allocated to Tamil Nadu.

[The Hindu’s supplied original account](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/tn-economic-performance-cannot-be-assessed-merely-by-gross-gst-revenue-commercial-taxes-minister/article71660464.ece) records the minister making this argument after Opposition parties criticised growth under the TVK government. The disagreement therefore involves both the choice of revenue measure and the broader conclusions drawn from it.

## September figures show the same divergence

An earlier commercial taxes department note, reported by [The New Indian Express on October 3](https://www.newindianexpress.com/amp/story/states/tamil-nadu/2026/Oct/03/tamil-nadus-gst-receipts-up-17-post-settlement), put September gross GST collections at ₹10,188 crore, down about 5%. Revenue retained after settlement, however, rose to ₹7,320 crore from ₹6,270 crore a year earlier, an increase of roughly 17%.

For April–September, that report cited retained receipts of ₹44,266 crore against ₹38,119 crore a year earlier, or about 16% growth. Those September and six-month figures cover different periods from the minister’s latest October and year-to-date percentages; they should not be treated as interchangeable measures.

## Rate changes add context

Tamilselvan also pointed to reductions in tax rates under the September 2025 GST rationalisation. The Union Finance Ministry’s [September 3, 2025 announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&lang=2&reg=3) documented reductions across numerous goods and recommended September 22 implementation for services and most goods, with specified exceptions.

That announcement supports the existence of the rate changes. It does not by itself quantify their contribution to Tamil Nadu’s October 2026 collections. The minister’s explanation should accordingly be distinguished from a measured breakdown of the causes of revenue growth or moderation.
