# [Lula reverses first-round setback to win Brazil’s presidential runoff](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/brazil-presidential-election-lula-bolsonaro/article71548352.ece)
*Updated: 2026-10-16T18:20:39+05:30*

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won reelection after trailing Flávio Bolsonaro in Brazil’s first presidential ballot. The runoff outcome supersedes the uncertainty surrounding Lula’s campaign in early October, while the right’s wider electoral gains remain central to the political landscape described in the original reporting.

![Image accompanying the original article](https://th-i.thgim.com/public/news/national/29f4pn/article71548508.ece/alternates/LANDSCAPE_1200/AFP__20261005__D2AP7KZ__v1__HighRes__BrazilElectionResults.jpg)

## The runoff changes the presidential picture

São Paulo’s regional electoral court reported Lula reelected following the October 25 vote. With 99.9% of ballot boxes counted, it put his national share at 51.77% of valid votes, against 48.23% for Bolsonaro. Geraldo Alckmin was also reelected vice-president. These percentages refer to the count reported by the court, rather than a separately verified final tally. [TRE-SP](https://www.tre-sp.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/eleicoes-2026-lula-e-reeleito-presidente-da-republica-confira-resultado-do-2o-turno-em-sp).

The result overturned the first-round order. The Superior Electoral Court recorded Bolsonaro at 47.03% and Lula at 45.16%, with 99.99% counted early on October 5. Neither won the majority needed to avoid a runoff. [Superior Electoral Court](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/flavio-bolsonaro-e-lula-vao-disputar-o-2o-turno-para-a-presidencia-da-republica).

## Why the first round mattered

The original Hindu account described a broader advance by Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party across legislative and state contests, extending beyond the presidential race. It argued that the party’s expansion drew heavily from traditional conservative and centre-right forces, rather than simply displacing Lula’s party. [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/brazil-presidential-election-lula-bolsonaro/article71548352.ece).

The same account identified abstention and coalition-building as urgent challenges for both presidential campaigns. Lula sought to bring nonvoters back to the polls; Bolsonaro aimed to consolidate anti-Workers’ Party sentiment and support from other right-wing candidates. Those were campaign strategies before the runoff, rather than evidence that either outcome was assured. [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/brazil-presidential-election-lula-bolsonaro/article71548352.ece).

The presidential contest ultimately produced a different result from its opening round. It does not, by itself, establish that the legislative gains described in the earlier account were reversed.
