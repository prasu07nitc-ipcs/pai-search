## Title

Hegseth faces expected U.S. House impeachment vote this week

## Description

Impeachment moves target Defense Secretary Hegseth as House vote looms amid GOP divisions and past scandals.

## Body

Pete Hegseth, the U.S. Defence Secretary, calls himself the Secretary of War, part of his effort to rebrand a military he said was too weak and “woke” when he took office last year. Now, with a House impeachment vote expected this week, he could soon share something in common with an actual secretary of war.

William Belknap, who held that title in 1876, became the first, and to date only, civilian leader of the U.S. military to be impeached by Congress.

U.S. Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced articles of impeachment on Monday (November 9, 2026), setting off a two-day clock for an expected House vote on charges including waging war against Iran without congressional approval and other abuses of power.

Congressional sources say the resolution may have the simple majority needed to pass the Republican-controlled House before Democrats take over on January 3. Conviction appears unlikely in the Senate, however, where a two-thirds majority is required and Republicans hold 53 of 100 seats.

House Speaker Mike Johnson opposed the effort, saying impeachment “in the middle of hostilities abroad” would be “a terrible thing for our allies and our enemies to see.”

Voter anger over the war helped Democrats win control of Congress in last week’s midterm elections, intensifying Republican divisions over a conflict that Vice-President J.D. Vance has publicly questioned and Mr. Hegseth has defended as “courageous and historic.”

Accused of taking huge kickbacks, Belknap handed President Ulysses Grant his resignation ahead of his scheduled impeachment vote and, according to a Senate history, “burst into tears.” The House impeached him anyway, but the Senate later acquitted him after falling short of the required two-thirds majority.

By contrast, Mr. Hegseth is promising to stay and fight.

#### Hegseth defiant

Mr. Hegseth has mounted a social media campaign, saying in a defiant video message last week that he would not back down. He also drew a comparison to President Donald Trump, who was impeached twice, saying: “It didn’t work against President Trump, and it won’t work with me.”

Asked if he had enough Republican support to defeat the impeachment push, Mr. Hegseth said on Monday (November 9, 2026): “We’re not worried about that.” He announced a Tuesday (November 10, 2026) press conference, his first at the Pentagon since May.

Mr. Trump has backed Mr. Hegseth and condemned the impeachment push in an interview with *CBS*’s “60 Minutes” broadcast on Sunday (November 8, 2026).

“They want to impeach him. Very unfair. We had two wars, we won both,” Mr. Trump said, according to a *CBS* transcript of the full interview.

Mr. Trump referred to both the Iran war, which is at an impasse, and to Venezuela, where the U.S. military deposed that country’s president, Nicolás Maduro, in a surprise January commando raid on his compound in the Venezuelan capital.

#### Defence spending at risk?

Some Republican lawmakers have expressed concern that if Mr. Hegseth stays, his strained relationship with Congress will undermine the Trump administration’s push for a $1.5 trillion defence spending package.

Mr. Massie, a Trump critic who is leaving office in January, warned in a statement on Monday (November 9, 2026) that impeachment would be “much gentler” than what’s in store for Mr. Hegseth once Democrats take control in January.

“They will hold hearings and trials. President Trump should replace Secretary Hegseth now while he can still get a replacement confirmed in the Senate,” Mr. Massie said.

Democrats have promised to investigate Mr. Hegseth over issues including the firing of the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and other senior leaders.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said on Sunday (November 8, 2026) he was a “hard yes” on a measure to impeach Mr. Hegseth.

Mr. Massie’s articles of impeachment accuse Mr. Hegseth of “high crimes and misdemeanours” ranging from waging the Iran war to various alleged abuses of power, including violating the free speech rights of U.S. Senator Mark Kelly, an Arizona Democrat.

Last year, Mr. Hegseth sought to prosecute Mr. Kelly after he and five other Democrats in Congress with backgrounds in the military and intelligence urged U.S. troops to refuse illegal orders. Mr. Hegseth accused them of sedition, something Mr. Trump at the time said in a social media post was “punishable by DEATH!”
