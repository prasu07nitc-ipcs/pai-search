# Morgan Smith

## Sequence: **3/27**
- Utterance: In December 2024, archaeologist and University of Tennessee at Chattanooga anthropology associate professor Morgan Smith found a hand-sized stone scraper dropped on dry land approximately 7,000 years ago, five miles off Florida's coast near a coral reef. He sifted 30 inches of sand and crushed shells to a blue-gray clay-silt layer formed on land; deeper digging with a student found 8,500-year-old prehistoric wood. The site is now eight feet underwater, and Smith described this as the first stone tool found buried in ancient soil beneath this part of the ocean. He is among nearly a dozen scientists in a five-year Marine Minerals Administration project surveying submerged sites. Despite increased research, he says comparatively little is known. He plans to return with students to excavate deeper layers that could hold older artifacts.
