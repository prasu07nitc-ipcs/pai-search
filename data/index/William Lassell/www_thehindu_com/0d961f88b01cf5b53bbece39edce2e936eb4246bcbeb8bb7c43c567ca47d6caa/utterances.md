# William Lassell

## Sequence: **6/23**
- Utterance: English astronomer William Lassell was born in Bolton, England, on June 18, 1799, into a fairly well-off family. He moved from Rochdale, Manchester, to Toxteth, Liverpool, in 1815 and apprenticed in a local merchant’s office until 1822. By November 1824, he had established himself as a beer brewer on Milton Street in Liverpool. His flourishing business provided wealth and leisure for astronomy. By summer 1839, he had established Starfield, an observatory at his home on West Derby Road, with a self-built 9-inch telescope. He visited Irish engineer and astronomer William Parsons, the third Earl Rosse, and returned to Liverpool with more knowledge. Lassell formed a partnership with William Rutter Dawes and James Nasmyth; the three were recognised as the Grand Amateurs of astronomy. They built a 24-inch telescope in his Starfield workshops, with a 20-foot tube and two-foot mirror polished by a machine of Lassell’s design. It was the first telescope of its size to achieve equatorial mounting, allowing objects to be tracked and observed longer. Using it, Lassell discovered Triton on October 10, 1846, 17 days after Neptune’s discovery; shared the independent discovery of Hyperion in 1848 with William Cranch Bond and George Phillips Bond; and discovered Ariel and Umbriel on October 24, 1851, bringing his satellite discoveries from two to four within five years. He later made more observations, built his third and final great telescope, a 48-inch-wide, 37-foot-long Newtonian reflector, and travelled between Liverpool and Malta for better viewing conditions. He received the Royal Astronomical Society Gold Medal in 1849 and served as its president from 1870 to 1872. He was elected a Royal Society fellow in 1849 and received its Royal Medal in 1858. He died in 1880, belonging to the last generation of great amateur astronomers whose work prepared the way for professional astronomers.
