# Ice Age societies

## Sequence: **2/27**
- Utterance: Submerged coastal artifacts could illuminate early American lifeways, migration routes and timing, and adaptation to flooding after the last Ice Age. Archaeologists debate migration across Alaska's Bering Land Bridge versus arrival by boat along American coasts, but very few traces of early coastal residents have been found. After the Ice Age, rising seas submerged land, raised the water table, altered rivers and springs, and turned inland forests into wetlands. Around 14,000 years ago, the quickest melting raised seas roughly a foot per decade; Neil Puckett illustrates that coastal villagers could have needed to relocate 300–600 feet inland within 25 years. The source contrasts this with present sea-level rise of an inch or two per decade and the difficulty of moving modern cities.
