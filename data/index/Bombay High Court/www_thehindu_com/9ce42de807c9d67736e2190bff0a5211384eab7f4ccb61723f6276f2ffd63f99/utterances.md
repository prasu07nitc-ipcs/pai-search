# Bombay High Court

## Sequence: **1/15**
- Utterance: On Tuesday, November 3, 2026, the Bombay High Court expressed deep pain and displeasure over controversy surrounding Dia Mirza’s video for the BMC’s eco-friendly, firecracker-free Deepavali campaign and directed the civic body to re-upload her clip, which had been deleted on Monday following objections from some BJP leaders and Mumbai Mayor Ritu Tawde. Taking suo motu notice, a bench of Girish Kulkarni and Neela Gokhale criticised a petty mindset, especially among representatives of ordinary people, and the unnecessary colour given to environmental awareness about air and noise pollution. It said no one could question the authorities’ choice of a celebrity, endorsed the BMC commissioner’s selection, and said BMC should not have deleted the video or reacted in this unwarranted knee-jerk manner. It cautioned objectors that everyone deserves respect regardless of religion, caste or creed and said the Constitution expects citizens to foster a secular spirit. It said Mumbai’s togetherness and strong sense of belonging must not be disturbed by loose comments, that it had not seen politicians or others who made remarks the previous day post awareness videos, and that everyone breathes the same air. It repeatedly expressed pain and displeasure, said such conduct was unexpected from public officeholders, and emphasised that the video’s message benefited everyone irrespective of religion or caste. It recalled that BMC acted pursuant to court orders banning firecrackers on public roads and streets throughout the year, not for a particular festival; Deepavali merely happened to be occurring. It noted firecrackers are also burst at marriages, processions, election victories and after cricket matches, and said its order covers all 365 days, with environment and control of air and noise pollution paramount. It said anyone may advocate for the environment, community spirit is most important, there is no scope for individualism, and society must nurture goodwill, togetherness and belonging instead of reprimanding people who come forward.
