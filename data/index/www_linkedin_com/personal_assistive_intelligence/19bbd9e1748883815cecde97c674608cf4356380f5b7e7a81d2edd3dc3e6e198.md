# Personal Assistive Intelligence

## Sequence: **2/16**
- Utterance: Personal Assistive Intelligence (PAI) is an architecture combining general-purpose AI models, goal-directed agents, operational harnesses, and evolving personal context. It carries relevant understanding across interactions and uses a person’s knowledge, preferences, goals, constraints, and decision principles to interpret requests and support action. Its personal core establishes relevance, portability carries that relevance across domains and platforms, and networkability draws on shared expertise and experience. The dimensions depend on each other: transferred context needs personal interpretation, shared expertise needs adaptation, and both depend on a harness for context, permissions, execution, and feedback. The individual contributes intent, expertise, experience, and judgment. Models and agents contribute reasoning, synthesis, planning, and action. The harness links these through memory, tools, orchestration, and oversight. The purpose is to extend human capability while preserving individual judgment and control. Success depends on how well the components improve the individual’s ability to understand situations, make decisions, and accomplish meaningful goals. PAI shifts the emphasis from isolated interactions to a continuing relationship between human judgment and machine capability. It requires meaningful memory, selective transfer, trustworthy knowledge exchange, and clear user control. Its promise is to carry forward what the individual has learned, adapt to what they need, and expand what they can accomplish through others’ knowledge and capabilities.
