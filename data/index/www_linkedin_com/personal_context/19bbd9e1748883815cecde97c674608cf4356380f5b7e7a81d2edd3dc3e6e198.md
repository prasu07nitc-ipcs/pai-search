# Personal context

## Sequence: **3/16**
- Utterance: Personal context comprises domain expertise, accumulated experience, preferences, priorities, constraints, and individual definitions of success. These differences affect which alternatives people consider, how they evaluate trade-offs, and what outcomes they find acceptable. PAI uses this context to shape reasoning and execution. Recommendations should reflect the user’s objectives and judgment, and actions should respect constraints and authority boundaries. Capturing, retrieving, and applying context can evolve without retraining the model. The challenge is to decide what matters, how long it remains valid, and where it applies. Instructions establish objectives, preferences, constraints, and expectations. Corrections expose differences between system assumptions and user intent. Explanations reveal rationales and trade-offs. For example, a vendor choice records an event, while prioritising delivery reliability over price captures a principle. Reliability may take precedence for critical supplies while price matters more for routine purchases. Context should preserve preferences and their conditions. It should remain inspectable, correctable, and under user control as preferences and circumstances change and prior interpretations prove incorrect. PAI improves through selective capture of meaningful context, preservation of rationale, and appropriate application.
