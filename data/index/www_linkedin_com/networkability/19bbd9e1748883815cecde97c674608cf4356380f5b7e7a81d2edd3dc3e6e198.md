# Networkability

## Sequence: **5/16**
- Utterance: Networkability extends personal intelligence through people, agents, knowledge sources, and reusable workflows. Expertise is distributed, and a personal system cannot derive all specialised knowledge from the user’s experience alone. Networked PAI helps people discover, evaluate, adopt, and adapt capabilities developed by others. A hypothetical business owner could combine an accounting professional’s reporting workflow, a procurement specialist’s supplier evaluation method, and a systems architect’s integration workflow. Scale, objectives, approval processes, budget, and current tools shape adaptation. Practical refinements may be contributed back with appropriate authorisation and validation. Shared expertise expands individual capability, and validated individual experience improves shared resources. Because networks can also spread unreliable assumptions and unsafe practices, they require clear sharing permissions and privacy boundaries, traceable authorship and provenance, and validation suited to intended use and consequences. Shared procurement methods should be kept separate from private contracts, prices, and priorities. Adopting a workflow leaves permissions and actions subject to individual controls. Under those conditions, participants gain access to broader expertise and validated contributions compound the value of shared resources.
