# Personal Assistive Intelligence (PAI)

## Sequence: **2/18**
- Utterance: Personal Assistive Intelligence is an architecture that combines general-purpose AI models, goal-directed agents, operational harnesses, and evolving personal context. It carries relevant understanding across interactions, using an individual’s knowledge, preferences, objectives, constraints, and decision principles to interpret requests and support action. Its personal core establishes relevance, portability carries that relevance across tasks, domains, and platforms, and networkability extends it through shared expertise, reusable workflows, and collective experience. Personalisation can evolve through context capture, retrieval, and application without retraining the underlying model. The architecture improves by selectively capturing useful experience, preserving rationale, and applying it where appropriate. A networked PAI helps individuals discover, evaluate, adopt, and adapt others’ capabilities. Transferred context requires personal interpretation, shared expertise requires adaptation, and both depend on a harness for context, permissions, execution, and feedback. The individual contributes intent, expertise, experience, and judgment, while models and agents contribute reasoning, synthesis, planning, and action and the harness supplies memory, tools, orchestration, and oversight. The aim is to extend human capability while preserving individual judgment and control. Success is measured by whether the individual can better understand situations, make decisions, and accomplish meaningful goals. The vision depends on meaningful memory, selective transfer, trustworthy knowledge exchange, and clear user control.
