# Election Commission of India

## Sequence: **1/21**
- Utterance: The Election Commission of India, Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and both Election Commissioners jointly filed a nearly 250-page affidavit in the Supreme Court late on October 26, 2026. They argued that restraining the CEC’s functioning would be contrary to law and that a CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge. The affidavit argued that media reports alleging internal friction and unilateral or authoritarian conduct were insufficient court evidence and that petitions based on such reports should not be maintainable. It denied releasing or authorising release of internal notes or files to news agencies. It said disagreements are resolved by majority under Section 18 of the 2023 appointments and service law. Decisions pass through both Election Commissioners and then the CEC, bear their signatures in the files, and are notified and implemented by the Secretariat; important functions remain with the Commission collectively. In urgent situations when members cannot attend in person, electronic approvals are obtained and later recorded. The affidavit said the July 28, 2026 internal allocation order was never implemented. It denied changing Form 6 through SIR orders dated June 24, 2025, October 27, 2025 and May 14, 2026, distinguishing an additional mapping declaration that assists voters and avoids further verification documents. Based on an October 22 communication from the West Bengal CEO, it said CEO officers filed 16.10 lakh tribunal appeals against voter inclusion under their statutory authority and Supreme Court orders, without bringing these matters to the Commission. Regarding 97 initially excluded Goa voters, it said house visits were conducted, 88 submitted Form 6 and their applications were accepted; six were abroad and three held Portuguese passports.
